\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Dataset and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We use AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026}, a Vietnamese VQA benchmark built on MS COCO images~\cite{lin2015coco}, in which each question has five short free-form answers and a reasoning type.
We remove 273 duplicated (image, question) pairs and 97 questions without a reasoning label. The
remaining 36{,}707 questions are split 70/15/15 by image (seed 42), so no test image is seen in
training. This gives 25{,}776 / 5{,}463 / 5{,}468 questions on 13{,}579 / 2{,}909 / 2{,}910
images. The split is stricter than a question-level split: it lowers the F1 of Global-only by
about 0.5 points.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Following AutoViVQA and ViMoE-VQA, we report Accuracy (exact match), word-level Precision, Recall
and F1, BLEU~\cite{papineni2002bleu}, ROUGE-L~\cite{lin2004rouge},
METEOR~\cite{banerjee2005meteor} and CIDEr~\cite{vedantam2015cider}, all against the five reference
answers. For each question, Precision, Recall and F1 compare the sets of words of the answer and of
the reference that gives the highest F1, and are then averaged over questions. Accuracy counts the answers identical to one of the references, ignoring case, and BLEU is
corpus-level BLEU-4 on whitespace tokens.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
The vision encoder, projector and decoder come from Vintern-1B-v3.5~\cite{doan2024vintern1b} and
stay frozen. We train with AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, effective batch size 8) on the
first reference answer. Bridges are trained for two epochs, after which the validation loss and
CIDEr stop improving. Models with a decoder LoRA are trained for one epoch, with the bridge, if
any, and the LoRA learned jointly from scratch. Global-only + LoRA is also reported after three
epochs, its best setting.
Decoding is greedy, with at most 50 new tokens, and every model fits on one 16\,GB NVIDIA T4. Each configuration is trained with three seeds
(42, 123, 3407), and we report the mean $\pm$ std. The token budget is selected on validation, and the test split is used only for the final report.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} compares with two groups. (1) Multimodal models fine-tuned on AutoViVQA: ViT5\_ViT,
BARTPhoBEiT~\cite{tran2023bartphobeitpretrainedsequencetosequenceimage}, Vintern-1B and
ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa}. (2) Zero-shot models: Vintern-1B, LLaMA~3.2, Gemini~2.0 and 2.5 Flash,
and GPT-5. Results of groups (1) and (2) are taken from the AutoViVQA and ViMoE-VQA papers. The
fine-tuned Vintern-1B uses the official recipe: six $448\times448$ tiles plus a thumbnail for
every AutoViVQA test image, and a rank-16 LoRA on the language model for one epoch. In the
analysis, we also use \emph{Global-only}, the bridge of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge} with $g=8$ and no
local tokens, without and with a decoder LoRA (\emph{Global-only + LoRA}). Our models are \emph{ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36)}, with $g=14$ and a frozen decoder, and
\emph{Local tokens + LoRA}, the 36 local tokens with a decoder LoRA (Section~\ref{sec:lora}).

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares ViBridge-VQA with the baselines. Without updating any weight of
Vintern-1B, ViBridge-VQA raises its F1 from 17.55 (zero-shot) to 54.56. This is comparable to the fine-tuned Vintern-1B (53.76), even on our stricter image-disjoint split
(Section~\ref{sec:data}), while the decoder reads 50 instead of 1{,}792 visual tokens and only
1.35\% of the parameters are trained. Only Accuracy is lower (11.75 versus 13.01). On test, the bridge alone with the same 14 global
tokens reaches 51.02 F1 ($\pm$0.10), so the 36 local tokens add 3.54 F1.

When the decoder may be adapted, the 36 local tokens with a decoder LoRA reach 58.06 F1 with
0.23\% of the parameters trained (Section~\ref{sec:lora}). They give the best Accuracy (14.37),
BLEU (22.40), ROUGE-L (56.11) and METEOR (49.23). ViMoE-VQA, which trains its fusion module and
decoder on AutoViVQA, keeps the best Precision and F1.

Results are stable across seeds: on test, the std is at most 0.19 for F1 and 0.95 for CIDEr, and test F1 is within 0.4 of validation F1.

Table~\ref{tab:bridges} compares three sources of visual tokens. \emph{Residual} (one token) and
\emph{Global-only} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge}, $g=8$) read the \texttt{[CLS]} vector. Five bridges learn
to compress the 577 encoder tokens: \emph{Tile-Attention} (self-attention and eight learned queries),
the light and full Q-Former~\cite{li2023blip2} (7 and 15 queries, two and four layers, the full one
also reading the question), C-Abstractor~\cite{cha2024honeybee} (convolution blocks around a
$3\times3$ pooling, 9 tokens instead of the 144 or more of Honeybee) and \emph{Patch-pool} (a linear
projection averaged into eight bands). All are randomly initialised, as a new connector would be
for a low-resource task, and trained with the same recipe. The third source is our local tokens,
alone or with global tokens (ViBridge-VQA).

\paragraph{Reusing the projector works best.} Without LoRA, the learned compressions stay 1.9--5.6 F1 below Global-only, despite up to
$9.4\times$ more parameters. Patch-pool, a minimal baseline that averages bands of consecutive encoder tokens after a newly
learned projection, reaches 45.78 F1. It differs from our local tokens in the pooling
(eight 1D bands instead of a 2D grid) as well as in the projection. In contrast, adding 36 or
144 local tokens to Global-only ($g=8$) raises F1 by 3.58 and 5.41 (Table~\ref{tab:budget}).
The projector also wins at a similar number of tokens: 17 tokens ($g=8$, $k=9$) give 52.82 F1,
against 46.84 for the 16-token full Q-Former, and 9 tokens ($g=8$, $k=1$) give 51.03, against
48.86 for the 9-token C-Abstractor. We
also tried to distil the projector into Global-only. A cosine loss between the mean tokens of the bridge and of the projector gives 50.35 F1, and a KL
loss between the answer distributions obtained with each gives 50.46. Neither reaches the 50.74 obtained without distillation. Without any trained part, the 36 local
tokens give only 27.55 F1, as the frozen model answers in long sentences (24.8 words on average).

\paragraph{Local tokens matter more than global tokens.} Table~\ref{tab:budget} and
Fig.~\ref{fig:results}a vary $g\in\{8,14\}$ and $k\in\{1,9,36,144\}$. At $g=14$, going from 1 to

\paragraph{Selected budget.} F1 grows with every added token, so all configurations with local
tokens lie on the accuracy--token Pareto front. Its knee (Section~\ref{sec:budget}) is $g=14$,
$k=36$ for every metric and also with generation time as the cost. TOPSIS and the post-hoc knee
on test agree. $k=144$ is 1.65 F1 higher, but $k=36$ obtains 72\% of its gain over Global-only
(67\% over the 14-token bridge of Table~\ref{tab:gsweep}) with 32\% of its tokens.

Table~\ref{tab:lora} adds a decoder LoRA. Without local tokens, LoRA brings every bridge to
52.69--53.47 validation F1, below ViBridge-VQA with a frozen decoder. Three epochs bring

54.96 F1.

\subsection{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Reasoning types.} Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b breaks down validation F1 by reasoning
type. Local tokens help most when the answer is visible in the image: at $g=8$, 36 local tokens
add 10.3 F1 on recognition, 5.0 on spatial and 3.7 on action questions. On these types, the
frozen-decoder model even beats Global-only + LoRA. Decoder adaptation helps more on causal and relational questions, which have the longest
answers (5.0 and 5.6 words), and on the 66 yes/no questions. ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36) + LoRA is the best of the four models on seven of the eight types,
all but yes/no.

\paragraph{Error analysis.} On validation, our answers are as long as the references (4.3 words on average), and most of them (77--81\%) partially overlap the closest reference. Compared with Global-only,
ViBridge-VQA raises exact matches (F1 = 1) from 9.7\% to 13.3\%. It also lowers answers with no word
in common with any reference from 9.6\% to 8.0\% (6.8\% with LoRA).

Table~\ref{tab:ood} tests the models on three Vietnamese VQA datasets not used for training.
ViTextVQA~\cite{nguyen2025vitextvqa} focuses on scene text, while ViVQA-X~\cite{vivqax2025} and
ViVQA~\cite{tran2021vivqa} are general-domain datasets on COCO images. About 10\% of their sampled
questions concern images of our training split, with different questions. On the general-domain datasets, ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36) outperforms Global-only + LoRA and the
zero-shot Vintern-1B even without LoRA (F1 24.91 versus 14.52 on ViVQA-X, 42.68 versus 23.90 on
ViVQA), and LoRA raises its F1 to 27.64 and 44.76.
Scene text needs more local tokens. With 36 of them, the model stays far below Vintern-1B (15.39
versus 32.01 F1), whereas ViBridge-VQA ($k$=144) is on par with it (33.26) while reading one view
instead of up to seven, suggesting that the gap comes mainly from the token budget. ViBridge-VQA
($k$=144) is also the best or on par on the general-domain datasets. Averaged over the three
datasets, F1 is 35.4 for ViBridge-VQA ($k$=144) and 29.3 for ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36) + LoRA,
against 23.5 for Vintern-1B.
