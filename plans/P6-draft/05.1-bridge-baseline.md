# 5.1 Bridge-architecture comparison (leak-free split)

We train each of five bridge modules on the grouped 70/15/15 split (§4.1),
seed 42, 4 epochs, distillation off, all other components frozen. Numbers below
are on the **validation split (5,463 questions)**, epoch 1 (metrics converge by
epoch 2; see §4.3). Generation metrics are computed at the **corpus level with
the standard pycocoevalcap implementations** (CIDEr-D, BLEU-4, ROUGE-L), so they
are directly comparable to the values reported by prior work on this benchmark.
Token-level Precision / Recall / F1 are per-sample, max over the five references,
then averaged.

## 5.1.1 Bridge suite (val)

| Bridge | Trainable params | % | CIDEr-D | BLEU-4 | BLEU-1 | ROUGE-L | Prec | Rec | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Multi-Token** (8 pooled tok) | 7.35 M | 0.78 | **94.4** | **19.58** | **54.79** | **50.00** | **51.1** | **52.4** | **44.2** |
| Full Q-Former (16 query) | 69.4 M | 6.91 | 86.7 | 17.46 | 51.52 | 47.09 | 47.9 | 49.8 | 43.3 |
| Light Q-Former (8 query) | 27.6 M | 2.87 | 83.8 | 16.81 | 50.83 | 45.99 | 46.7 | 49.0 | 42.9 |
| Tile-Attention (8 tok) | 4.14 M | 0.44 | 82.7 | 16.32 | 50.26 | 46.09 | 46.6 | 49.2 | 43.0 |
| Residual (1 tok) | 4.86 M | 0.52 | 56.3 | 8.13 | 34.15 | 35.95 | 34.0 | 43.8 | 37.6 |

Multi-token is best on every metric while training only 0.78 % of parameters —
more parameter-efficient than the q-formers by an order of magnitude. The
single-token residual bridge is far behind: representing the image with one
vector is the binding constraint, not bridge depth. This reproduces, on a
leak-free split, the multi-token advantage reported in the earlier bridge study.

## 5.1.2 Against prior work on AutoViVQA

Prior published numbers on AutoViVQA (as reported by the respective papers /
the ViMoE-VQA study). Our multi-token bridge, val split, corpus metrics:

| Model | F1 | BLEU | ROUGE | METEOR | CIDEr |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Vintern-1B (base) | 17.55 | 1.91 | 25.84 | 23.93 | 8.54 |
| Vintern-1B (fine-tuned) | 53.76 | 6.11 | 51.93 | 35.25 | 72.84 |
| BARTPhoBEiT | 45.88 | 4.33 | 44.83 | 24.57 | 188.96¹ |
| GPT-5 (zero-shot) | 50.89 | 6.07 | 47.30 | 33.34 | 84.20 |
| ViMoE-VQA (MoE, 5-seed avg) | **60.69** | 12.54 | 47.07 | **39.10** | 88.67 |
| **Multi-Token Bridge (ours)** | 44.2 | **19.58** | **50.00** | 28.45² | **94.4** |

¹ BARTPhoBEiT's CIDEr is an outlier driven by verbose generation; the ViMoE-VQA
study attributes the BARTPhoBEiT/ViMoE CIDEr gap to abbreviated-answer behaviour
(≈4.4 generated tokens vs 5.6 reference). Our bridge shows the *same* concise
generation (mean 4.4 words), which is consistent with its high CIDEr-D and lower
METEOR.

² METEOR is highly implementation-sensitive: our in-house token-metric library
reports 41.1 for the same predictions, above ViMoE's 39.1, while the
pycocoevalcap multi-reference METEOR reports 28.5. We therefore do not draw a
METEOR comparison and report both numbers for transparency.

**Reading.** The multi-token bridge — 7 M trainable parameters over two frozen
backbones — exceeds the ViMoE-VQA MoE on the three implementation-stable
generation metrics: CIDEr-D (+5.7), BLEU-4 (+7.0), ROUGE-L (+2.9). It trails
ViMoE on token-level F1 (−16). We read this as: the bottleneck for
answer–reference token overlap is the frozen Qwen2-0.5B decoder (ViMoE trains a
six-layer decoder from scratch with label smoothing, which fits reference
phrasing more tightly), not the visual bridge. The bridge contribution is a
generation-quality gain at a fraction of the trainable cost; it is orthogonal to
this paper's routing question and is included as a controlled baseline.

## 5.1.3 Caveats

- **Single seed.** These numbers are seed 42 only. ViMoE-VQA reports a 5-seed
  average (42, 123, 3407, 2026, 8668) with std ≤ 0.16. A camera-ready version
  needs either the same 5-seed protocol for at least the multi-token row, or a
  paired-bootstrap argument over the 5,463-sample val set (which we do have).
- **Cross-paper metric implementations.** The earlier AutoViVQA bridge table used
  an in-house token-metric library; we switch to corpus pycocoevalcap here for
  cross-paper comparability, so our numbers differ slightly from that table
  (e.g. CIDEr 94.4 here vs 99.9 there).
- **Validation, not test.** Test-split numbers are reported once, in §5.
