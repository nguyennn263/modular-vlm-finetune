\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

We formulate VQA as conditional answer generation: given an image $I$ and a
Vietnamese question $q$, the model generates a short free-form answer
$y=(y_1,\dots,y_T)$. ViBridge-VQA starts from Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b},
keeps all of its pre-trained weights frozen, and gives its decoder two kinds of visual
tokens: global tokens from a small trainable bridge and local tokens from the frozen
projector of Vintern-1B. Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} contrasts this design with the official
fine-tuning recipe of Vintern-1B.

\subsection{Visual Encoding}
\label{sec:encoding}

The vision encoder is InternViT-300M (24 layers, width 1024) and the language
decoder is Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct (24 layers, width 896). Each image is resized to a
single $336\times336$ view, which InternViT splits into $24\times24$ patches of
$14\times14$ pixels. The encoder returns one 1024-dimensional vector per patch and a
global \texttt{[CLS]} vector $v\in\mathbb{R}^{1024}$. Vintern-1B discards $v$; it
merges every $2\times2$ block of neighbouring patch vectors into one 4096-dimensional
vector (pixel shuffle) and maps each merged vector into the decoder input space with
a two-layer MLP projector $P$ that was pre-trained to align the two backbones. At our
resolution this yields a $12\times12$ grid of 144 region tokens.

\subsection{Global and Local Tokens}
\label{sec:bridge}

\paragraph{Global tokens.}
A small trainable bridge $g_\phi$ expands the \texttt{[CLS]} vector into $g$ tokens
with two linear heads:
\begin{equation}
  g_\phi(v) = \big[\,W_a v + b_a;\ \mathrm{reshape}_{(g-1)\times 896}(W_e v + b_e)\,\big]
  \in \mathbb{R}^{g\times 896}.
  \label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
The first head produces an \emph{anchor} token and the second $g-1$ complementary
tokens that present the global image content to the frozen decoder from different
learned directions. For $g=14$ the bridge has 12.86M parameters.

\paragraph{Local tokens.}
The local tokens are the 144 region tokens of the frozen projector $P$, average-pooled
over the $12\times12$ grid to an $s\times s$ grid, so that $k=s^2$ with
$s\in\{1,3,6,12\}$; $k=144$ keeps every region token and $k=36$ averages each
$2\times2$ block. Pooling the output of $P$, rather than learning a new projection of
the patches, keeps the local tokens in the representation the decoder was pre-trained
to read. The $k$ tokens replace the embeddings of $k$ image-context placeholders in
Vintern-1B's own image slot, \texttt{<img>}\,$\cdots$\,\texttt{</img>}, inside the
prompt, while the $g$ global tokens are placed before the prompt.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}

Only $\phi$ is trained; the vision encoder, the projector and the decoder remain
frozen, and no adapter is added to the decoder. We minimise the answer
cross-entropy
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = -\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p\big(y_t \mid y_{<t},\, g_\phi(v),\, \mathrm{pool}_s(P(x)),\, q\big),
  \label{eq:ce}
\end{equation}
where $x$ are the merged patch vectors. With $g=14$, 12.86M parameters (1.35\%) are
trainable. For comparison we also report the \emph{global-only} model ($k=0$, the
Multi-Token bridge with $g=8$) and its variant trained jointly with a rank-16 decoder
LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} on the query, key, value and output projections (2.16M
additional parameters).

\subsection{Choosing the Token Budget}
\label{sec:budget}

The decoder cost grows with the number of visual tokens $g+k$, so we treat $(g,k)$ as
a budget. Accuracy rises with every token step in our experiments, so every
configuration lies on the accuracy--cost Pareto front and the front alone does not
select one. We therefore pick its \emph{knee}~\cite{satopaa2011kneedle}: after
normalising cost $c$ and accuracy $a$ to $[0,1]$ over the front, the knee maximises
$\hat a - \hat c$, the height above the chord between the cheapest and the most
accurate configuration. We compute it on the validation split only and check it with
TOPSIS~\cite{hwang1981topsis} under several weightings of accuracy and cost.
