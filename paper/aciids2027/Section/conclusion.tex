\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we introduced \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a parameter-efficient approach that
improves the Vietnamese VLM Vintern-1B for generative visual question answering without
updating any of its pre-trained weights. Instead of adapting the model on many high-resolution tiles, ViBridge-VQA changes what its
frozen decoder sees: trainable global tokens from the \texttt{[CLS]} vector and local tokens
from the pre-trained projector. By selecting their number as the knee of the accuracy--cost Pareto front, we obtained a single
configuration, 14 global and 36 local tokens, that is the knee for every metric and both
evaluation splits. Experiments on AutoViVQA show that this configuration outperforms the officially fine-tuned Vintern-1B on seven of eight metrics while reading up to 36 times fewer visual tokens, and that
combining it with a lightweight decoder LoRA yields the highest accuracy, ROUGE-L and METEOR
among the compared systems. Our analyses show that local tokens matter far more than global ones
and complement decoder adaptation.

Our study also has limitations. ViMoE-VQA, a model trained specifically for AutoViVQA,
still achieves a higher F1, and the published baselines were evaluated on a different split
of AutoViVQA than ours. Out of distribution, the local tokens allow a single
$336\times336$ view to match the six-tile Vintern-1B on the scene-text ViTextVQA, but
Vintern-1B remains clearly better on OpenViVQA. Finally, accuracy had not saturated at the 144 region tokens available at our resolution;
higher-resolution inputs, which we plan to explore, could bring further gains, especially
for reading text.

Overall, our findings show that an existing Vietnamese VLM can be improved substantially by
choosing carefully which visual tokens its frozen decoder receives, and that the number of
these tokens can be set with an explicit accuracy--cost criterion. The code and the grouped split are available at
\url{https://github.com/nguyennn263/modular-vlm-finetune}. We hope that this work will
encourage efficient and carefully evaluated multimodal models for low-resource languages.
