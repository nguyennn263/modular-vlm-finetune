\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work presents \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a parameter-efficient method that improves
the Vietnamese VLM Vintern-1B for generative visual question answering. By keeping
the vision encoder and the language decoder frozen and training only a lightweight
multi-token bridge jointly with a rank-16 decoder LoRA, ViBridge-VQA updates 1.01\%
of the parameters and encodes each image once. On AutoViVQA it raises the F1 of
Vintern-1B from 17.6 to 54.5, outperforms the officially fine-tuned Vintern-1B on
seven of eight metrics with up to six times fewer image tiles, achieves the best
generation scores among all compared systems, and transfers to other Vietnamese VQA
datasets. Our component-wise analysis shows that adapting the decoder is the key
ingredient: it improves every bridge design and makes them perform almost equally,
whereas larger bridges and representation alignment do not help.

ViMoE-VQA, a model trained specifically for AutoViVQA, still achieves a higher F1,
and the baselines were reported on a different split of AutoViVQA. Future work will
add patch-level visual tokens through the pre-trained projector of Vintern-1B while
keeping a single view, and study larger Vietnamese decoders. The code and the grouped split
are available at \url{https://github.com/nguyennn263/modular-vlm-finetune}.

Overall, ViBridge-VQA shows that an existing Vietnamese VLM can be improved
substantially by adapting a small fraction of its parameters, and that a careful
component-wise analysis indicates where further effort is best spent. We hope this
work will encourage efficient and well-evaluated multimodal models for low-resource
languages.
