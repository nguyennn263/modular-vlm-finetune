\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Dataset and Metrics}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We use AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026}, built on MS COCO images~\cite{lin2015coco}, where each
question has five short free-form answers and a reasoning type. After removing 273 duplicated
(image, question) pairs and 97 questions without a reasoning label, we split the remaining
36{,}707 questions 70/15/15 by image (seed 42), so no test image is seen in training: 25{,}776 /
5{,}463 / 5{,}468 questions on 13{,}579 / 2{,}909 / 2{,}910 images. This split is stricter than a
question-level one: it lowers the F1 of Global-only by about 0.5 points.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Following AutoViVQA and ViMoE-VQA, we report Accuracy, word-level Precision, Recall and F1,
BLEU~\cite{papineni2002bleu}, ROUGE-L~\cite{lin2004rouge}, METEOR~\cite{banerjee2005meteor} and
CIDEr~\cite{vedantam2015cider} against the five references. Precision, Recall and F1 compare the
word sets of the answer and of the reference with the highest F1, and are averaged over questions.
Accuracy counts answers identical to a reference, ignoring case; BLEU is corpus-level BLEU-4 on
whitespace tokens.

\subsection{Setup}

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
The encoder, projector and decoder come from Vintern-1B-v3.5~\cite{doan2024vintern1b} and stay
frozen. We train with AdamW (learning rate $2\times10^{-4}$, effective batch size 8) on the first
reference answer. Bridges are trained for two epochs, after which the validation loss and CIDEr
stop improving. Models with a decoder LoRA are trained for one epoch, with the bridge (if any) and
the LoRA learned jointly from scratch; Global-only + LoRA is also reported after three epochs, its
best setting, which Table~\ref{tab:ood} and Fig.~\ref{fig:types} use. Decoding is greedy with at
most 50 new tokens, and every model fits on one 16\,GB NVIDIA T4. Each configuration is trained
with three seeds (42, 123, 3407), four for Global-only (adding 2026), and tables report mean
$\pm$ std. The token budget is selected on validation; the test split is used only for the final
report.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} compares with two groups of models: (1) fine-tuned on AutoViVQA:
ViT5\_ViT, BARTPhoBEiT~\cite{tran2023bartphobeitpretrainedsequencetosequenceimage}, Vintern-1B and
ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa}, and (2) zero-shot: Vintern-1B, LLaMA~3.2, Gemini~2.0 and 2.5
Flash, and GPT-5. Their results are taken from the AutoViVQA and ViMoE-VQA papers. The fine-tuned
Vintern-1B follows the official recipe: six $448\times448$ tiles plus a thumbnail per test image
and a rank-16 LoRA on the language model for one epoch. The analysis also uses
\emph{Global-only}, the bridge of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge} with $g=8$ and no local tokens, with and
without a decoder LoRA. Our models are \emph{ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36)}, with $g=14$, and
\emph{ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36) + LoRA}.

\paragraph{ViBridge-VQA matches the fine-tuned Vintern-1B.}
Without updating any weight of Vintern-1B, ViBridge-VQA raises its F1 from 17.55 (zero-shot) to
54.56 (Table~\ref{tab:main}). This is comparable to the fine-tuned Vintern-1B (53.76), even on our
stricter split, while the decoder reads 50 instead of 1{,}792 visual tokens and only 1.35\% of the
parameters are trained. Only Accuracy is lower (11.75 versus 13.01). On test, the bridge alone
with the same 14 global tokens reaches 51.02 F1 ($\pm$0.10), so the 36 local tokens add 3.54 F1.

\paragraph{LoRA adds 3.6 F1.}
With a decoder LoRA, ViBridge-VQA reaches 58.17 F1 (+3.61) with 1.57\% of the parameters trained,
and gives the best Accuracy (14.67), BLEU (22.53), ROUGE-L (56.25) and METEOR (49.32). ViMoE-VQA,
which trains its fusion module and decoder on AutoViVQA, keeps the best Precision and F1. Results
are stable: on test, the std is at most 0.19 for F1 and 0.95 for CIDEr, and test F1 is within 0.4
of validation F1.

Table~\ref{tab:bridges} compares three sources of visual tokens. \emph{Residual} (one token) and
\emph{Global-only} (Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge}, $g=8$) read the \texttt{[CLS]} vector. Five bridges
learn to compress the 577 encoder tokens: \emph{Tile-Attention} (self-attention and eight learned
queries), the light and full Q-Former~\cite{li2023blip2} (7 and 15 queries, two and four layers;
the full one also reads the question), C-Abstractor~\cite{cha2024honeybee} (convolution blocks
around a $3\times3$ pooling, 9 tokens instead of the 144 or more of Honeybee) and
\emph{Patch-pool} (a linear projection averaged into eight bands). All are randomly initialised, as
a new connector would be for a low-resource task, and trained with the same recipe. The third
source is our local tokens, alone or with global tokens (ViBridge-VQA).

\paragraph{Reusing the projector works best.}
The learned compressors stay 1.9--5.6 F1 below Global-only, despite up to $9.4\times$ more
parameters. Patch-pool, a minimal baseline that averages bands of encoder tokens after a newly
learned projection, reaches 45.78 F1; it differs from our local tokens in the pooling (1D bands
instead of a 2D grid) and in the projection. In contrast, adding 36 or 144 local tokens to
Global-only ($g=8$) raises F1 by 3.58 and 5.41 (Table~\ref{tab:budget}). The projector also wins at
a similar number of tokens: 17 tokens ($g=8$, $k=9$) give 52.82 F1 against 46.84 for the 16-token
full Q-Former, and 9 tokens ($g=8$, $k=1$) give 51.03 against 48.86 for the 9-token C-Abstractor.
Distilling the projector into Global-only does not help: a cosine loss between the mean tokens of
the bridge and of the projector gives 50.35 F1, and a KL loss between their answer distributions
50.46, both below the 50.74 obtained without distillation. Without any trained part, the 36 local
tokens give only 27.55 F1, as the frozen model answers in long sentences (24.8 words on average).

\paragraph{Local tokens matter more than global tokens.}
Table~\ref{tab:budget} and Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b vary $g\in\{8,14\}$ and $k\in\{1,9,36,144\}$.
At $g=14$, going from 1 to 144 local tokens adds 4.94 F1 and 19.05 CIDEr, whereas going from 8 to
14 global tokens adds only 0.2--0.8 F1 at a fixed $k$. The gain per token falls from 0.07 F1 (23 to
50 tokens) to 0.015 F1 (50 to 158 tokens).

\paragraph{The knee is $g=14$, $k=36$.}
Since F1 grows with every added token, all configurations with local tokens lie on the
accuracy--token Pareto front. Its knee (Section~\ref{sec:budget}, Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b) is
$g=14$, $k=36$ for every metric, and also with generation time as the cost; TOPSIS and a post-hoc
knee on test agree. $k=144$ is 1.65 F1 higher, but $k=36$ obtains 72\% of its gain over
Global-only (67\% over the 14-token bridge of Table~\ref{tab:gsweep}) with 32\% of its tokens.

\paragraph{Global tokens peak at $g=14$.}
Table~\ref{tab:gsweep} and Fig.~\ref{fig:results}a vary $g$ from 4 to 18 for Global-only.
Validation F1 rises from 48.98 ($g=4$) to 50.74 ($g=8$) and 51.56 ($g=14$), then falls to 51.20
and 51.26 at $g=16$ and 18, and CIDEr follows the same trend. We therefore combine the local tokens
with $g=14$, and keep $g=8$ as the Global-only reference.

\paragraph{One view cuts the encoder cost 13 times.}
The decoder reads 50 visual tokens instead of 1{,}792 for the official tiling, and the encoder
processes one $336\times336$ view (191 GMACs) instead of seven $448\times448$ views (362 GMACs
each). Within our budgets, $k=144$ makes generation about 15\% slower than $k=36$.

\paragraph{LoRA gains more with local tokens.}
ViBridge-VQA + LoRA is the best model on every metric of Table~\ref{tab:lora} (58.17 F1, 122.88
CIDEr), and LoRA gains more on ViBridge-VQA than on Global-only for all eight metrics (+3.61 versus

\paragraph{Local tokens help most on visible content.}
Fig.~\ref{fig:types}a breaks down validation F1 by reasoning type. ViBridge-VQA adds 11.0 F1 over
Global-only on recognition, 6.4 on action and 5.8 on spatial questions, and beats Global-only +
LoRA on these types without adapting the decoder. Decoder adaptation helps more on causal and
relational questions, which have the longest answers (5.0 and 5.6 words), and on the 66 yes/no
questions. ViBridge-VQA + LoRA is the best of the four models on seven of the eight types, all but
yes/no.

\paragraph{More exact answers, fewer misses.}
On validation, answers are as long as the references (4.3 words on average), and most of them
(77--81\%) partially overlap the closest reference. Compared with Global-only, ViBridge-VQA raises
exact matches (F1 = 1) from 9.7\% to 13.3\% and lowers answers with no word in common with any
