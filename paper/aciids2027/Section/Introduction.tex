\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Visual Question Answering (VQA) asks a model to answer a natural-language question about an
image. Large vision--language models (VLMs) such as BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2},
Flamingo~\cite{alayrac2022flamingo} and LLaVA~\cite{liu2023visualinstructiontuning} generate
free-form answers by connecting a pre-trained vision encoder to a pre-trained language model. For
Vietnamese, the open Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b} connects an InternViT-300M
encoder~\cite{chen2024internvl} to a Qwen2.5-0.5B decoder~\cite{qwen2025qwen25}, and benchmarks
such as ViVQA~\cite{tran2021vivqa} and the reasoning-oriented AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026}
measure progress in this low-resource setting.

Adapting such a model to a new benchmark remains expensive. The fine-tuned Vintern-1B baseline of
AutoViVQA adds LoRA adapters to the language model and splits each test image into six
high-resolution tiles plus a thumbnail, so its decoder reads 1{,}792 visual tokens per image.
ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa} instead designs a task-specific mixture-of-experts fusion module. A
cheaper option keeps the VLM frozen and trains only a small connector, usually of one of two
designs. A \emph{global-only bridge} maps the global image vector to a few tokens: it is cheap but
loses small objects, printed text and spatial relations. A \emph{learned patch compressor}, such as
a Q-Former, keeps these details but must learn a new mapping to the decoder, which is hard with
limited Vietnamese data.

Vintern-1B offers a third option. Its projector is already pre-trained to map image regions into
the input space of the decoder, so this alignment need not be learned again. The model also
discards the global \texttt{[CLS]} vector of its encoder, which summarises the whole scene. We
therefore ask whether, with limited labelled data, reusing this alignment beats learning a new
connector, and how many global and local tokens the frozen decoder needs.

We propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, which feeds the frozen decoder of Vintern-1B two complementary
kinds of visual tokens from a single image view. \emph{Local} tokens reuse the frozen projector,
average-pooled to a chosen budget and placed in the original image slot, so the decoder reads
region features in the representation it was trained on. \emph{Global} tokens, produced from the
\texttt{[CLS]} vector by a small trainable bridge, add the scene-level information that Vintern-1B
ignores. By default, the bridge is the only trained part, so Vintern-1B stays unchanged and can be shared
across tasks. As every token adds inference cost, we treat the number of tokens as a budget and
select the knee of the accuracy--cost Pareto front. Without updating any weight of Vintern-1B,
ViBridge-VQA reaches an F1 close to that reported for its fine-tuned version, while feeding its
decoder 36 times fewer visual tokens. Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a global--local bridge that combines trainable global tokens with
  local tokens from the frozen projector of the VLM, optionally with a lightweight decoder LoRA.
  \item An explicit \textbf{accuracy--cost budget} for the number of visual tokens, selected on
  validation as the knee of the Pareto front.
  \item An \textbf{analysis of where the gains come from}: in our setup, reusing the pre-aligned
  projector is more data-efficient than learning a new connector, and beats learned connectors with up to $9.4\times$ more parameters (by 6.0 F1 over a
  Q-Former at a similar number of tokens); local tokens matter more than global ones; and they also
  help on two other Vietnamese VQA datasets.
\end{itemize}
