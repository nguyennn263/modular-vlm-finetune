\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

In this work, we introduced \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a parameter-efficient approach that
improves the Vietnamese VLM Vintern-1B for generative VQA without updating any of its
pre-trained weights, by giving its frozen decoder trainable global tokens from the
\texttt{[CLS]} vector and local tokens from the pre-trained projector. With 14 global and 36
local tokens, the knee of the accuracy--cost Pareto front on validation, training only the
bridge raises F1 on AutoViVQA from 17.6 to 54.6, above the published fine-tuned Vintern-1B on
seven of eight metrics, and training a lightweight decoder LoRA with it raises F1 to 58.2.
Local tokens matter far more than global ones and complement decoder adaptation.

Our study also has limitations. ViMoE-VQA still achieves a higher F1, and the published
baselines were evaluated on a different split. Our evidence is limited to Vintern-1B and the
Vietnamese benchmarks we tested, so it does not establish that the approach generalises in the
same way to other VLMs or languages, and the global tokens were only compared against eight of
them. Out of distribution, Vintern-1B remains clearly better on OpenViVQA, and accuracy had not
saturated at 144 local tokens, so higher-resolution inputs, which we plan to explore, could
help further, especially for reading text.

Overall, an existing Vietnamese VLM can be improved substantially by choosing which visual
tokens its frozen decoder receives, and how many, with an explicit accuracy--cost criterion.
The code and the grouped split are available at
\url{https://github.com/nguyennn263/modular-vlm-finetune}. We hope that this work will
encourage efficient and carefully evaluated multimodal models for low-resource languages.
