\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Visual Question Answering (VQA) is a multimodal task in which a model must
interpret an image, understand a natural-language question and produce an
answer. Recent progress has been driven by large vision--language models (VLMs)
such as BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2}, Flamingo~\cite{alayrac2022flamingo} and
LLaVA~\cite{liu2023visualinstructiontuning}, which pair a pre-trained vision encoder
with a pre-trained language model. For Vietnamese, Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b}
follows this design: it connects an InternViT-300M encoder~\cite{chen2024internvl}
to a Qwen2.5-0.5B decoder~\cite{qwen2025qwen25} through a small projector and is among
the strongest open Vietnamese VLMs. Benchmarks such as ViVQA~\cite{tran2021vivqa},
OpenViVQA~\cite{nguyen2023openvivqa} and, more recently, the reasoning-oriented
AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026} allow such models to be evaluated in a low-resource
setting.

Obtaining a strong model on a new Vietnamese benchmark, however, remains costly.
One option is to fine-tune an existing VLM: the fine-tuned Vintern-1B baseline of
AutoViVQA adds LoRA to the language model and encodes every image as up to six
high-resolution tiles plus a thumbnail, so that the decoder reads up to 1{,}792
visual tokens per image. Another option is to design a new architecture, as
ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa} does with a sparse mixture-of-experts fusion module and a
decoder trained on top of frozen encoders. A cheaper alternative is to keep the VLM
frozen and train only a small connector, but the choice of connector is delicate. A
connector that reads a single global image vector is light but cannot convey small
objects, text or positions, whereas connectors that learn to compress the patch
tokens, such as Q-Formers or learned pooling, must learn a new alignment to the
decoder from limited data. This raises the question: \emph{which visual tokens should
a frozen Vietnamese VLM receive, and how many?}

We propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, which answers this question with two complementary
kinds of tokens. Vintern-1B passes its decoder one token per image region through a
projector that was pre-trained to align the two backbones, but it discards the global
\texttt{[CLS]} vector of the encoder. ViBridge-VQA reuses that frozen projector to
produce \emph{local} tokens, average-pooled to a chosen budget and placed in
Vintern-1B's own image slot, and adds \emph{global} tokens produced from the
\texttt{[CLS]} vector by a small trainable bridge. The vision encoder, the projector
and the decoder remain frozen; only the global bridge is trained (1.35\% of the
parameters), and each image is encoded once at $336\times336$ pixels. Because more
tokens cost more computation, we treat the number of global and local tokens as a
budget and select it as the knee of the accuracy--cost Pareto front.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This work makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a global--local bridge that improves
  Vintern-1B without updating any of its weights. With 14 global and 36 local tokens
  it raises F1 on AutoViVQA from 17.6 (zero-shot) to 54.6 and outperforms the
  officially fine-tuned Vintern-1B on seven of eight metrics while passing 50 instead
  of up to 1{,}792 visual tokens; with all 144 local tokens it outperforms it on all
  eight metrics (F1 $+2.3$, CIDEr $+43.9$).
  \item We study the \textbf{visual token budget} over eight configurations and three
  seeds and select it with an explicit criterion: the knee of the Pareto front falls
  on the same configuration for all eight metrics, on validation and test, whether
  cost is counted in tokens or in measured inference time, and a multi-criteria
  decision analysis (TOPSIS) confirms the choice.
  \item We provide a \textbf{component-wise analysis}: local tokens matter far more
  than global ones and help most on perception-heavy questions; bridges that learn to
  compress the patches do not beat a bridge over the global vector; and decoder LoRA,
  which lifts every global-only bridge, is not needed once local tokens are present,
  as the frozen-decoder model surpasses our best decoder-LoRA model.
\end{itemize}
