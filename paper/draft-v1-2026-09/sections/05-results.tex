\section{Main Results}
\label{sec:results}

Freeze InternViT-300M and Qwen2-0.5B; train only the \multitoken{} bridge
(7.35\,M params, $0.78\%$). Optionally add a rank-16 LoRA on the decoder's
attention projections ($q/k/v/o$; 2.16\,M, $0.23\%$), for $1.01\%$ trainable in
total. One tile, grouped leak-free 70/15/15 split, greedy decoding.

\subsection{Recipe vs.\ prior work}
\label{sec:results-main}

Table~\ref{tab:main-inhouse} reports all AutoViVQA baselines and our recipe: the
left block is the eight in-house metrics ($\times 100$, matching the benchmark's
table), the right block is corpus pycocoevalcap CIDEr-D / BLEU-4 / ROUGE-L
(implementation-stable, for cross-paper comparison; available only for our rows
and ViMoE). Even the \textbf{bridge alone} (no LoRA) beats the fully fine-tuned
Vintern-1B on BLEU, METEOR and CIDEr and is within ${\sim}4$ points on ROUGE-L;
adding the $0.23\%$ decoder LoRA at 3 epochs puts the recipe above Vintern-FT on
\emph{every} generation metric --- at ${\sim}1\%$ of the trainable parameters, no
backbone fine-tuning, and one tile instead of up to twelve. The recipe also beats
ViMoE-VQA on every generation and corpus metric, but trails it on token-F1
($-11.2$ bridge alone, $-6.0$ at 3 epochs) and Vintern-FT on Acc.

A bootstrap over the $5{,}463$ val samples (\texttt{scripts/bootstrap\_ci.py})
puts \multitoken{}-plain's corpus CIDEr-D at $92.5$, $95\%$ CI $[89.9, 95.3]$ ---
the whole interval above ViMoE's $88.7$, so the generation win is not a lucky
draw. The F1 gap is likewise real ($60.7$ vs.\ our CI $[48.9, 50.3]$); ViMoE
publishes no per-sample predictions, so these are one-sample intervals. The
recipe's own corpus numbers are val above; on \textbf{held-out test} they are
CIDEr-D $101.3$ / $104.8$ (1 / 3 epochs) --- within $2$ points of the val
figures. Sec.~\ref{sec:ablation} diagnoses the F1 gap.

\subsection{Held-out test}
\label{sec:results-corpus}

On the held-out test split ($n = 5{,}468$, 4 seeds), \multitoken{}-plain scores
F1 $49.20$ / CIDEr $93.24$ vs.\ val $49.55$ / $96.49$ --- a gap of $-0.35$ F1 that
is \emph{not consistent in direction} across bridges (\bridge{mini\_qformer} test
$47.25$ vs.\ val $47.05$; \bridge{residual} $45.49$ vs.\ $45.91$;
\bridge{qformer} test $46.62$ vs.\ val $47.13$). The full recipe holds up too:
$+$LoRA test F1 $53.15$ / $54.28$ (1 / 3 epochs) vs.\ val $53.52$ / $54.71$
($\Delta \approx -0.4$), corpus CIDEr-D $101.3$ / $104.8$. The recipe is not
over-fit to val.

\subsection{Compute-efficiency of the tile lever}
\label{sec:abl-compute}

\ntiles{} is a genuine compute lever (Table~\ref{tab:compute}): dynamic range
$1{\rightarrow}6$ is FLOPs $\times 6.0$, latency $\times 4.0$, throughput
$\times 5.2$. The recipe spends \textbf{none} of it. Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-tiles}
shows why spending it is actively harmful; this is the FLOPs/latency analysis
ViMoE-VQA explicitly deferred.
