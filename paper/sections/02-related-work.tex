\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Vietnamese VQA and AutoViVQA.}
Vietnamese VQA has moved from extractive, single-word datasets (ViVQA,
OpenViVQA, ViTextVQA) toward free-form generative answering.
\textbf{AutoViVQA}~\cite{autovivqa} provides 19{,}411 images and 37{,}077
questions, each with five diverse free-form answers and a reasoning-type label,
evaluated on Accuracy, word-level P/R/F1, BLEU, ROUGE-L, METEOR and CIDEr.
Published results span seq2seq baselines (ViT5+ViT, BARTPhoBEiT), a fine-tuned
\textbf{Vintern-1B}, proprietary LLMs, and \textbf{ViMoE-VQA}. AutoViVQA ships
only an 80/20 train/val division with no public test split.

\paragraph{Frozen-backbone VLMs and the vision--language bridge.}
A large body of work adapts a frozen image encoder and a frozen language model by
training only a small connector: \textbf{Frozen}~\cite{frozen} and
\textbf{BLIP-2}~\cite{blip2} (a Q-Former of learned query tokens),
\textbf{LLaVA}~\cite{llava} (an MLP projector, but with the LLM un-frozen during
instruction tuning). Vintern-1B follows the LLaVA-style recipe but, when adapting
downstream, fully fine-tunes the InternViT encoder and the projector and
LoRA-tunes the decoder. We keep \emph{everything} frozen except a lightweight
bridge, and study five bridge designs spanning a capacity ladder.
``Inference-Optimal VLMs''~\cite{inferenceoptimalvlms} argues that, at a fixed
compute budget, a smaller LLM with more visual tokens often beats a larger LLM
with fewer; our tile and bridge ablations probe the frozen-backbone version of
that trade-off and find the frozen decoder, not the visual token budget, to be
the binding constraint.

\paragraph{Parameter-efficient adaptation, and where in the decoder.}
Parameter-efficient methods insert a few trainable weights: \textbf{adapters}
\cite{adapters}, \textbf{prefix / prompt tuning}~\cite{prefixtuning}, and
\textbf{LoRA}~\cite{lora}. Two questions the LoRA literature leaves partly open,
and that we address for a frozen-backbone VLM, are \emph{where} in the decoder the
adapter should go and how its benefit interacts with the choice of connector.
Interpretability work distinguishes the sub-layers --- feed-forward layers as
key--value memories of lexical associations~\cite{ffnkvmemory}, attention as
inter-position routing --- and adapter-placement studies report attention-only
adaptation is often competitive with adapting all linear layers. Our result --- a
decoder-attention LoRA lifts token-F1 while a same-rank feed-forward LoRA
destabilises training, and once the attention LoRA is present the five bridges
become nearly interchangeable --- is a concrete data point for frozen 0.5\,B
decoders on a low-resource generative VQA task.

\paragraph{Mixture-of-experts and ``reasoning-aware'' routing.}
\textbf{ViMoE-VQA}~\cite{vimoevqa} improves AutoViVQA accuracy by building a new
generative model (frozen CLIP ViT-B/32 and PhoBERT, fusion layers, a four-expert
Top-2 MoE, a six-layer decoder, trained from scratch) and attributes part of its
gain to a router that ``implicitly enables approximate reasoning-aware expert
selection''. We are a direct counterpoint on the \emph{same benchmark}: we
improve the existing Vintern-1B rather than replace it, and we test the
reasoning-aware premise directly (Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-routing}) --- reasoning type
carries no signal about which visual-compute action is optimal, and no learned
policy beats a fixed one. ViMoE's own leave-one-out ablation is consistent
(removing any expert moves BLEU by only $0.11$--$0.44$), suggesting the MoE gain
is better explained by added capacity than by specialisation. ViMoE also defers a
FLOPs/latency characterisation, which Sec.~\ref{sec:abl-compute} supplies.
