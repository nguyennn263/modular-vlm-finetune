\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work presents \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, which improves the Vietnamese VLM
Vintern-1B for generative visual question answering without updating any of its
weights. ViBridge-VQA passes the frozen decoder two kinds of visual tokens from a
single image view: global tokens, produced from the \texttt{[CLS]} vector by a small
trainable bridge, and local tokens, produced by the frozen pre-trained projector of
Vintern-1B and pooled to a chosen budget. Selecting that budget as the knee of the
accuracy--cost Pareto front gives 14 global and 36 local tokens for every metric and
both evaluation splits. With 1.35\% of the parameters trained and 50 visual tokens, it
raises the F1 of Vintern-1B on AutoViVQA from 17.6 to 54.6 and outperforms the
officially fine-tuned Vintern-1B on seven of eight metrics; with all 144 local tokens
it outperforms it on all eight. Our analysis shows that local tokens matter far more
than global ones, that the pre-trained projector should provide them rather than a
newly learned compression of the patches, and that a frozen decoder with such tokens
surpasses a decoder adapted with LoRA over global tokens only.

ViMoE-VQA, a model trained specifically for AutoViVQA, still achieves a higher F1, and
the baselines were reported on a different split of AutoViVQA. Accuracy had not
saturated at the 144 region tokens available at our resolution, the decoder-LoRA
model remains better on causal and relational questions, and the global--local model
has not yet been evaluated on other Vietnamese VQA datasets, in particular scene-text
ones, where local tokens should matter most. Future work will therefore combine the
local tokens with decoder adaptation, raise the image resolution, and evaluate
ViBridge-VQA out of distribution. The code and the grouped split are available at
\url{https://github.com/nguyennn263/modular-vlm-finetune}.

Overall, ViBridge-VQA shows that an existing Vietnamese VLM can be improved
substantially by choosing which visual tokens its frozen decoder receives, and that
the size of this budget can be chosen with an explicit accuracy--cost criterion. We
hope this work will encourage efficient and well-evaluated multimodal models for
low-resource languages.
