\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Visual Question Answering (VQA) is a multimodal task in which a model must
interpret an image, understand a natural-language question and produce an
answer. Recent progress has been driven by large vision--language models (VLMs)
such as BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2}, Flamingo~\cite{alayrac2022flamingo} and
LLaVA~\cite{liu2023visualinstructiontuning}, which pair a pre-trained vision encoder
with a pre-trained language model. For Vietnamese, Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b}
follows this design: it connects an InternViT-300M encoder~\cite{chen2024internvl}
to a Qwen2.5-0.5B decoder~\cite{qwen2025qwen25} and is among the strongest open
Vietnamese VLMs. Benchmarks such as ViVQA~\cite{tran2021vivqa},
OpenViVQA~\cite{nguyen2023openvivqa} and, more recently, the reasoning-oriented
AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026} allow such models to be evaluated in a low-resource
setting.

Obtaining a strong model on a new Vietnamese benchmark, however, remains costly.
One option is to fine-tune an existing VLM: the fine-tuned Vintern-1B baseline of
AutoViVQA follows the official recipe of Vintern, which freezes the vision encoder
and the projector, adds LoRA to the language model and encodes every image as up
to six high-resolution tiles, so that visual encoding dominates the cost. Another
option is to design a new architecture, as ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa} does with a
sparse mixture-of-experts fusion module and a transformer decoder trained on top
of frozen encoders. Both options are expensive in different ways, and neither
shows which component of the model actually drives the improvement. This
motivates two questions: \emph{can Vintern-1B be improved on a new benchmark while
its backbones stay frozen and only a small fraction of its parameters is trained,
and which components matter for this improvement?}

To address these questions, we propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a parameter-efficient
method that improves Vintern-1B for generative Vietnamese VQA. ViBridge-VQA keeps
the vision encoder and the language decoder frozen, replaces the original projector
with a lightweight \emph{multi-token bridge} that maps a global image vector into
the decoder input space, and trains it jointly with a low-rank adapter
(LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}) on the decoder. Only 1.01\% of the parameters are trained,
and each image is encoded as a single view instead of several tiles. On AutoViVQA,
ViBridge-VQA outperforms the officially fine-tuned Vintern-1B on seven of eight
metrics while spending far less visual computation. We further analyse which
components produce this gain.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This work makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a frozen-backbone method that improves
  Vintern-1B by training a 7.35M-parameter multi-token bridge and a rank-16 decoder
  LoRA (1.01\% of all parameters). On AutoViVQA it raises F1 from 17.6 (zero-shot)
  to 54.5, outperforms the officially fine-tuned Vintern-1B on seven of eight
  metrics with up to six times fewer image tiles, achieves the best generation
  scores among all compared systems, and transfers to other Vietnamese VQA
  datasets.
  \item We present a \textbf{component-wise analysis} of the method. Adapting the
  decoder is the key ingredient: it improves every bridge design and reduces their
  F1 spread from 5.6 to 0.8 points, whereas larger bridges, multi-reference
  training and alignment to the original projector do not improve F1. A design
  study of the bridge (five architectures, pooling operators, number of tokens)
  shows that a small bridge over the global image vector gives the best trade-off
  between accuracy and size.
  \item We provide a \textbf{thorough evaluation}: an image-grouped split without
  leakage, multiple seeds with bootstrap confidence intervals, the cost of each
  tile setting, gains per reasoning type showing the largest improvements on
  reasoning-intensive questions, and an out-of-distribution study.
\end{itemize}
