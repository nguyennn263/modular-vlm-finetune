\begin{abstract}
Vision--language models (VLMs) have advanced Visual Question Answering (VQA)
substantially, yet adapting them to Vietnamese benchmarks remains costly: the
usual options are to fine-tune an existing VLM on high-resolution multi-tile inputs
or to design a new architecture.
In this work, we propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a parameter-efficient adaptation
of the Vietnamese VLM Vintern-1B for generative VQA. Both pre-trained backbones,
the InternViT-300M vision encoder and the Qwen2.5-0.5B language decoder, are kept
frozen. A lightweight multi-token bridge maps a single global view of the image
into the decoder, and a rank-16 LoRA adapts the decoder, so that only 1.01\% of the
parameters are trained. On the AutoViVQA benchmark, ViBridge-VQA reaches a token
F1 of 54.52 and a CIDEr of 108.43 on a held-out test split. It matches the
fine-tuned Vintern-1B on F1 and clearly exceeds it, as well as zero-shot large
language models, on all generation metrics, while still trailing the
mixture-of-experts model ViMoE-VQA on F1. A systematic ablation shows that larger
bridges and representation alignment do not help, whereas decoder adaptation
helps every bridge design and makes them perform almost equally. \ifoodtext Evaluation on
four further Vietnamese VQA datasets shows that ViBridge-VQA matches or exceeds
the zero-shot Vintern-1B on general VQA but reveals a clear limitation on reading
scene text.\else Evaluation on two
further general-domain Vietnamese VQA datasets shows that ViBridge-VQA transfers
beyond its training benchmark, matching or exceeding the zero-shot Vintern-1B.\fi
