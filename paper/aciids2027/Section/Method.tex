\section{Proposed Method}
\label{sec:method}

We formulate VQA as conditional answer generation: given an image $I$ and a Vietnamese question
$q$, the model generates a short free-form answer $y=(y_1,\dots,y_T)$. ViBridge-VQA keeps every
pre-trained weight of Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b} frozen and changes only the visual input
of its decoder.

\subsection{Architecture}
\label{sec:encoding}

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}, ViBridge-VQA keeps the three components of Vintern-1B: the
InternViT-300M vision encoder (24 layers, width 1024), its two-layer MLP projector $P$ and the
Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct decoder (24 layers, width 896). Each image is resized to one
$336\times336$ view, i.e., $24\times24$ patches of $14\times14$ pixels. InternViT, pre-trained at
$448\times448$, bicubically interpolates its position embeddings to this resolution. The encoder returns one vector per
patch and a global \texttt{[CLS]} vector $v\in\mathbb{R}^{1024}$. Vintern-1B uses only the patch
vectors: pixel shuffle~\cite{chen2024internvl15} merges each $2\times2$ block of patches into one
4096-dimensional vector, and $P$, pre-trained to align the two backbones, maps it into the decoder
input space. This yields a $12\times12$ grid of 144 region tokens. ViBridge-VQA uses both outputs
of the encoder.

\subsection{Global Tokens}
\label{sec:bridge}

The \texttt{[CLS]} vector summarises the whole scene, but Vintern-1B never passes it to the
decoder. A small trainable bridge $f_\phi$ expands it into $g$ tokens with two linear heads:
\begin{equation}
  f_\phi(v) = \big[\,W_a v + b_a;\ \mathrm{reshape}_{(g-1)\times 896}(W_e v + b_e)\,\big]
  \in \mathbb{R}^{g\times 896},
  \label{eq:bridge}
\end{equation}
where $W_a\in\mathbb{R}^{896\times1024}$ and $W_e\in\mathbb{R}^{(g-1)896\times1024}$. The first
head gives an \emph{anchor} token, and the second gives $g-1$ complementary tokens that present the
global content along different learned directions. The bridge has $1025\cdot896\cdot g$
parameters (12{,}857{,}600 for $g=14$), and its tokens are placed before the prompt. When the
bridge alone is varied from 4 to 18 tokens, F1 peaks at $g=14$
(Section~\ref{sec:budget-results}), the value used in ViBridge-VQA. Our Global-only baseline uses
$g=8$, matching the eight output tokens of Tile-Attention and the light Q-Former
(Section~\ref{sec:rq-source}).

\subsection{Local Tokens}
\label{sec:local}

To give the decoder region-level detail, ViBridge-VQA also passes local tokens taken directly from
the 144 region tokens of $P$. We average-pool the $12\times12$ grid to an $s\times s$ grid, so
$k=s^2$ with $s\in\{1,3,6,12\}$: $k=144$ keeps every region token, and $k=36$ averages each
$2\times2$ block. The pooled tokens replace the embeddings of $k$ placeholders inside Vintern-1B's
own image slot, \texttt{<img>}\,$\cdots$\,\texttt{</img>}. Because they come from the pre-trained
projector rather than a new projection, they stay in the representation that the decoder was
trained to read, and they need no parameters.

\subsection{Training}
\label{sec:training}

By default, only the bridge parameters $\phi$ are trained; the encoder, the projector and the
decoder stay frozen. We minimise the cross-entropy of the answer tokens:
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L} = -\sum_{t=1}^{T} \log p\big(y_t \mid y_{<t},\, f_\phi(v),\, \mathrm{pool}_s(P(x)),\, q\big),
  \label{eq:ce}
\end{equation}
where $x$ denotes the merged patch vectors. Optionally, ViBridge-VQA is combined with decoder
adaptation: a rank-16 LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} on the query, key, value and output projections of
every decoder layer, trained jointly with the bridge, adds 2{,}162{,}688 parameters. Percentages of
trained parameters are relative to all model parameters (the frozen 0.94B plus the trained ones).
With $g=14$, the bridge trains 12.86M parameters (1.35\%), and the bridge with LoRA 15.02M
(1.57\%).

\subsection{Budget Selection}
\label{sec:budget}

The cost of the decoder grows with the number of visual tokens $g+k$, so we treat the pair $(g,k)$
as a budget. Among the configurations with local tokens, F1 increases with every added token in our
experiments, so all of them lie on the accuracy--token Pareto front, which alone does not single
one out. We therefore select its \emph{knee}~\cite{satopaa2011kneedle}, after which additional tokens bring
diminishing returns. With accuracy $a$ the validation F1 and cost $c$ the number of visual tokens,
both normalised to $[0,1]$ over the front, the knee maximises $\hat a - \hat c$, i.e., it lies
furthest above the line joining the cheapest and the most accurate configuration. We check the
choice with TOPSIS~\cite{hwang1981topsis}, which weighs accuracy (F1, CIDEr, METEOR) and cost
(tokens, evaluation time, trained parameters, seed variance) equally. Both criteria use validation
only, and the code with all rankings is released with the paper.
