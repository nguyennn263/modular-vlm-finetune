\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Dataset and Evaluation Metrics}
\label{sec:data}

\paragraph{Dataset.}
We use AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026}, a Vietnamese VQA benchmark on MS COCO
images~\cite{lin2015coco} with five short free-form reference answers and a reasoning type per
question. We split it by image, so that no test image is seen in training: after removing 273
duplicated (image, question) pairs and 97 questions without one of the eight reasoning labels,
the 36{,}707 questions are divided 70/15/15 by image (seed 42) into 25{,}776 / 5{,}463 / 5{,}468
questions on 13{,}579 / 2{,}909 / 2{,}910 images. This split is stricter: it lowers the F1 of
Global-only by about 0.5 points compared with a split that does not group questions by image.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
Following AutoViVQA and ViMoE-VQA, we report Accuracy (exact match), word-level Precision, Recall
and F1, BLEU~\cite{papineni2002bleu}, ROUGE-L~\cite{lin2004rouge},
METEOR~\cite{banerjee2005meteor} and CIDEr~\cite{vedantam2015cider} against the five reference
answers.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Implementation details.}
The vision encoder, the projector and the decoder are taken from
Vintern-1B-v3.5~\cite{doan2024vintern1b} and kept frozen. We use AdamW (learning rate
$2\times10^{-4}$, effective batch size 8) and train on the first reference answer: two epochs for
ViBridge-VQA, after which neither the validation loss nor CIDEr improves, and one joint epoch for
the variants with a decoder LoRA; Global-only + LoRA is also trained for three epochs, its best
setting (+1.3 validation F1). Decoding is greedy (at most 50 new tokens). Each configuration is
trained with three seeds (42, 123, 3407), and we report the mean $\pm$ std. Every model fits on a
single 16\,GB NVIDIA T4. The token budget is selected on validation; the test split is used only
for the final report.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
We compare with (1) multimodal models fine-tuned on AutoViVQA: ViT5\_ViT,
BARTPhoBEiT~\cite{tran2023bartphobeitpretrainedsequencetosequenceimage}, Vintern-1B and
ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa}; (2) zero-shot models: Vintern-1B, LLaMA~3.2, Gemini~2.0 and 2.5 Flash,
and GPT-5; and (3) \emph{Global-only}, the Multi-Token bridge of Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge} with $g=8$
and no local tokens, without and with a decoder LoRA (\emph{Global-only + LoRA}). Results of
groups (1) and (2) are taken from the AutoViVQA and ViMoE-VQA papers; the fine-tuned Vintern-1B
follows the official recipe (up to six $448\times448$ tiles plus a thumbnail, rank-16 LoRA on the
language model for one epoch). Our models are \emph{ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36)}, the selected budget ($g=14$,
$k=36$), and \emph{ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36) + LoRA}.

Table~\ref{tab:main} compares ViBridge-VQA with the baselines. Without updating any weight of
Vintern-1B, ViBridge-VQA raises its F1 from 17.55 (zero-shot) to 54.56, comparable to the
fine-tuned Vintern-1B (53.76), while its decoder reads 50 instead of 1{,}792 visual tokens and only
1.35\% of the parameters are trained; only its Accuracy is lower (11.75 versus 13.01). A decoder
LoRA (1.57\% of the parameters trained) raises F1 to 58.17 (+3.6) and gives the best Accuracy,
BLEU, ROUGE-L and METEOR. ViMoE-VQA, whose fusion module and decoder are trained on AutoViVQA,
keeps the best Precision and F1. With the same one-epoch LoRA, ViBridge-VQA is 4.7 validation F1
above Global-only (58.21 versus 53.47; Table~\ref{tab:bridges}). Results are stable across seeds:
on test, the std is at most 0.19 for F1 and 0.95 for CIDEr, and test F1 is within 0.6 of
validation F1.

Table~\ref{tab:bridges} compares three sources of visual tokens: the \texttt{[CLS]} vector
(\emph{Residual}, one token, and \emph{Multi-Token}, Eq.~\eqref{eq:bridge}), learned compressions
of the patches (\emph{Tile-Attention} with eight queries, light and full
Q-Former~\cite{li2023blip2}), and our local tokens from the pre-trained projector.

\paragraph{Reusing the projector works best.} Without decoder adaptation, the learned compressions
stay 3.7--5.6 F1 below Multi-Token despite up to $9.4\times$ more parameters, whereas adding 36 or
144 local tokens to the same bridge ($g=8$) raises F1 by 3.6 and 5.4 (Table~\ref{tab:budget}).
Training Global-only to imitate the projector does not close the gap: aligning its mean output
token with the projector's (cosine) or its answer distribution with the one obtained from the
projector's tokens (KL) gives 50.35 and 50.46 F1, against 50.74.

\paragraph{Local tokens and LoRA are complementary.} With a decoder LoRA, every bridge without
local tokens reaches 52.69--53.47 F1, still below ViBridge-VQA with a frozen decoder (54.96). The
same LoRA adds 3.3 F1 to ViBridge-VQA, more than the 2.7 it adds to Multi-Token.

\paragraph{Local tokens matter more than global tokens.} At $g=14$, going from 1 to 144 local
tokens adds 4.9 F1 and 19.1 CIDEr (Table~\ref{tab:budget}, Fig.~\ref{fig:results}a), whereas
going from 8 to 14 global tokens adds 0.2--0.8 F1 at a fixed $k$. The gain per token falls from
0.07 F1 between 23 and 50 tokens to 0.015 between 50 and 158 tokens.

\paragraph{Selected budget.} Since F1 grows with every added token, all configurations with local
tokens lie on the accuracy--token Pareto front. Its knee (Section~\ref{sec:budget}) is $g=14$,
$k=36$ for every metric, and also with evaluation time as the cost (times differ by only minutes
below $k=144$). TOPSIS with equal weights and the knee on the test split (a post-hoc check) give
the same budget. $k=144$ is 1.7 F1 higher, but $k=36$ obtains 72\% of its gain over Global-only
with 32\% of its tokens. Against the 14-token Global-only bridge ($g=14$, $k=0$), the 36 local
tokens add 3.4 F1 (51.56 to 54.96), 67\% of the gain of $k=144$.

\paragraph{Number of global tokens.} Varying the Multi-Token bridge from $g=4$ to $g=20$ (three
seeds; two for $g=20$), validation F1 rises from 49.0 ($g=4$) to 50.7 ($g=8$) and 51.6 ($g=14$),
then stays at 51.2--51.6 for $g=16$--$20$.

\paragraph{Computational cost.} The decoder reads 50 visual tokens instead of the 1{,}792 of the
official tiling (seven views for every AutoViVQA test image), and the encoder processes one
$336\times336$ view (191 GMACs) instead of seven $448\times448$ views (362 GMACs each), 13 times
less computation. Within our budgets, $k=144$ instead of 36 makes evaluation about 17\% slower.

\subsection{Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\paragraph{Reasoning types.} Local tokens help most when the answer depends on visible content
(Fig.~\ref{fig:results}b): at $g=8$, 36 local tokens add 10.3 F1 on recognition, 5.0 on spatial and
3.7 on action questions, where this frozen-decoder model also beats Global-only + LoRA. Decoder
adaptation helps more on causal and relational questions, which have the longest answers (5.0 and
5.6 words), and on yes/no questions. ViBridge-VQA ($k$=36) + LoRA is the best of the four models on
seven of the eight types, all but yes/no.

\paragraph{Error analysis.} Our answers are as long as the references (4.3 words on average), and
77--81\% of them partially overlap the closest reference. Compared with Global-only, ViBridge-VQA
raises exact matches (F1 = 1) from 9.7\% to 13.3\% and lowers answers sharing no word with any
reference from 9.6\% to 8.0\% (6.8\% with LoRA).
