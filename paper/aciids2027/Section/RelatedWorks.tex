\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Vietnamese VQA.}
Vietnamese VQA datasets include ViVQA~\cite{tran2021vivqa}, synthetic
ViCLEVR~\cite{tran2023viclevr}, book-cover ViOCRVQA~\cite{pham2024viocrvqa}, and scene-text
ViTextVQA~\cite{nguyen2025vitextvqa}. AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026} contains 37{,}077 questions
over 19{,}411 COCO images~\cite{lin2015coco}, generated by large language models, filtered by
validators, and labelled with reasoning types. On the modelling side,
BARTPhoBEiT~\cite{tran2023bartphobeitpretrainedsequencetosequenceimage} and
AViVQA-TranConI~\cite{nguyen2024tranconi} combine pre-trained visual and Vietnamese language
representations, Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b} brings an instruction-tuned VLM to
Vietnamese, and ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa} routes multimodal tokens to sparse experts for
generative VQA on AutoViVQA. AutoViVQA and ViMoE-VQA are prior work from our group; we use the
former but no component of the latter. The method, image-disjoint split, and all results here are
new. The approaches above train a new model or fine-tune a VLM on multi-tile inputs,
whereas we keep Vintern-1B frozen and use a single image view.

\paragraph{Connectors.}
Connectors between frozen vision encoders and language models differ in the mapping they learn.
Frozen~\cite{tsimpoukelli2021frozen} learns a visual prefix from a global image representation.
BLIP-2's Q-Former~\cite{li2023blip2} compresses patch features with learned queries, but is a
188M-parameter module pre-trained on millions of image--text pairs.
LLaVA~\cite{liu2023visualinstructiontuning,liu2024llava15} preserves locality by projecting every
patch token, at the cost of hundreds of decoder tokens. Honeybee~\cite{cha2024honeybee} proposes
locality-preserving abstractors, while InternVL~\cite{chen2024internvl}, Vintern-1B's basis,
merges neighbouring patches before projection. All learn the patch-to-decoder mapping during
large-scale pre-training. ViBridge-VQA instead reuses that mapping and trains only a 12.9M-parameter
global bridge on roughly 26k task questions.

\paragraph{Visual token budget.}
The number of visual tokens largely determines decoder cost. Inference-optimal scaling
of VLMs~\cite{li2024inferenceoptimal} shows that a few tokens can suffice for many tasks, and
Matryoshka multimodal models~\cite{cai2024matryoshka} show with nested pooled budgets that the
required budget depends on the task. Other work reduces the tokens directly:
DeCo~\cite{yao2024deco} replaces the Q-Former with adaptive average pooling of the patch tokens,
TokenPacker~\cite{li2024tokenpacker} learns a compact projector,
LLaVA-PruMerge~\cite{shang2024prumerge} and FastV~\cite{chen2024fastv} prune or merge the tokens
of an existing model, and VisionZip~\cite{yang2025visionzip} keeps those that receive the most
attention from \texttt{[CLS]}. Like DeCo, we use average pooling, but apply it to the output of an
already pre-trained projector while keeping the entire VLM frozen. We select the budget using
explicit criteria:
the knee of the Pareto front~\cite{satopaa2011kneedle} and TOPSIS~\cite{hwang1981topsis}.
Parameter-efficient methods such as adapters~\cite{houlsby2019adapters}, prefix
tuning~\cite{li2021prefix} and LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora} reduce the cost of adaptation from the other
side, by training a few additional weights in the decoder. We use decoder LoRA as a baseline and as
a complement.
