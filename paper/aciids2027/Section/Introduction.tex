\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Visual Question Answering (VQA) is a multimodal task in which a model must
interpret an image, understand a natural-language question and produce an
answer. Recent progress has been driven by large vision--language models (VLMs)
such as BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2}, Flamingo~\cite{alayrac2022flamingo} and
LLaVA~\cite{liu2023visualinstructiontuning}, which pair a pre-trained vision encoder
with a pre-trained language model. For Vietnamese, Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b}
follows this design: it connects an InternViT-300M encoder~\cite{chen2024internvl}
to a Qwen2.5-0.5B decoder~\cite{qwen2025qwen25} and is among the strongest open
Vietnamese VLMs. Benchmarks such as ViVQA~\cite{tran2021vivqa},
OpenViVQA~\cite{nguyen2023openvivqa} and, more recently, the reasoning-oriented
AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026} allow such models to be evaluated in a low-resource
setting.

Obtaining a strong model on a new Vietnamese benchmark, however, remains costly.
One option is to fine-tune an existing VLM: the fine-tuned Vintern-1B baseline of
AutoViVQA follows the official recipe of Vintern, which freezes the vision encoder
and the projector, adds LoRA to the language model and encodes every image as up
to six high-resolution tiles, so that visual encoding dominates the cost. Another
option is to design a new architecture, as ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa} does with a
sparse mixture-of-experts fusion module and a transformer decoder trained on top
of frozen encoders. Neither option indicates which component of an existing VLM
actually limits its accuracy. This motivates two questions: \emph{can a strong
Vietnamese VLM be adapted with both of its backbones frozen and only a small
fraction of its parameters trained, and, if a gap remains, where is the
bottleneck?}

To address these questions, we propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a parameter-efficient
adaptation of Vintern-1B for generative Vietnamese VQA. ViBridge-VQA keeps the
vision encoder and the language decoder frozen and replaces the original
projector with a lightweight \emph{multi-token bridge} that maps a single global
view of the image into the decoder input space, and a low-rank adapter
(LoRA~\cite{hu2022lora}) on the decoder is trained jointly with the bridge. Only 1.01\% of the parameters are
trained, and the visual encoder processes one image view instead of several
tiles. We further analyse where the remaining gap comes from by varying one
component at a time: the capacity of the bridge, the training signal of the
bridge, and the capacity of the decoder.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
This work makes the following contributions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item We propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a frozen-backbone adaptation of Vintern-1B
  that trains a 7.35M-parameter multi-token bridge and a rank-16 decoder LoRA
  (1.01\% of all parameters). On AutoViVQA it matches the fine-tuned Vintern-1B on
  F1, clearly exceeds it on all generation metrics, and is stable across seeds and
  between the validation and test splits.
  \item We present a \textbf{systematic bottleneck analysis}. Larger bridges,
  multi-reference training and alignment to the original projector do not improve
  F1, whereas decoder adaptation improves every bridge design and reduces their
  F1 spread from 4.4 to 0.9 points. The remaining bottleneck therefore lies in the
  frozen decoder rather than in the visual pathway.
  \item We provide a \textbf{reliable evaluation}: an image-grouped split without
  leakage, multiple seeds with bootstrap confidence intervals, the cost of each
  tile setting, an analysis by reasoning type, and an out-of-distribution study on
  \ifoodtext four Vietnamese VQA datasets that exposes a clear limitation on
  scene-text questions\else two further general-domain Vietnamese VQA
  datasets\fi.
\end{itemize}
