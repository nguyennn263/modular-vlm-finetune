\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Visual Question Answering (VQA) asks a model to answer a natural-language question about an
image. Large vision--language models (VLMs) such as BLIP-2~\cite{li2023blip2},
Flamingo~\cite{alayrac2022flamingo} and LLaVA~\cite{liu2023visualinstructiontuning} generate
free-form answers by connecting a pre-trained vision encoder to a pre-trained language model. For
Vietnamese, the open Vintern-1B~\cite{doan2024vintern1b} connects an InternViT-300M
encoder~\cite{chen2024internvl} to a Qwen2.5-0.5B decoder~\cite{qwen2025qwen25}, and benchmarks
such as ViVQA~\cite{tran2021vivqa} and the reasoning-oriented AutoViVQA~\cite{autovivqa2026}
measure progress in this low-resource setting.

Adapting a VLM to a new benchmark remains expensive. The fine-tuned Vintern-1B baseline in
AutoViVQA adds LoRA adapters and splits each test image into six high-resolution tiles plus a
thumbnail, yielding 1{,}792 visual tokens per image. ViMoE-VQA~\cite{vimoevqa} instead introduces
a task-specific mixture-of-experts fusion module. A cheaper alternative freezes the VLM and trains
a small connector. A \emph{global-only bridge} maps the global image vector to a few tokens, but
loses small objects, printed text, and spatial relations. A \emph{learned patch compressor}, such
as a Q-Former, retains these details but must learn a new mapping to the decoder from limited
Vietnamese data.

Vintern-1B offers a third option: its projector already maps image regions into decoder input
space, so this alignment need not be learned again. It also discards the encoder's global
\texttt{[CLS]} vector, which summarises the whole scene. We therefore ask whether reusing this
alignment is more effective than learning a new connector with limited labelled data, and how many
global and local tokens the frozen decoder needs.

We propose \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, which feeds the frozen decoder of Vintern-1B two complementary
kinds of visual tokens from a single image view. \emph{Local} tokens are obtained from the frozen
projector, average-pooled to a selected budget, and inserted into the image slot, preserving the
representation the decoder was trained to read. \emph{Global} tokens, produced from
\texttt{[CLS]} by a small trainable bridge, provide scene-level information that Vintern-1B
otherwise ignores. By default, only the bridge is trainable, leaving Vintern-1B unchanged and
reusable across tasks. Because tokens add inference cost, we treat their count as a budget and
select the knee of the accuracy--cost Pareto front. Without updating Vintern-1B, ViBridge-VQA
achieves an F1 close to that reported for the fine-tuned model while using 36 times fewer visual
tokens.
Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{ViBridge-VQA}, a global--local bridge that combines trainable global tokens with
  local tokens from the frozen projector of the VLM, optionally with a lightweight decoder LoRA.
  \item An explicit \textbf{accuracy--cost budget} for the number of visual tokens, selected on
  validation as the knee of the Pareto front.
  \item An \textbf{analysis of the source of the gains}: in our setup, reusing the pre-aligned
  projector is more data-efficient than learning a new connector and outperforms learned connectors
  with up to $9.4\times$ more parameters (by 6.0 F1 over a Q-Former at a similar token count).
  Local tokens contribute more than global tokens and also help on two other Vietnamese VQA datasets.
\end{itemize}
